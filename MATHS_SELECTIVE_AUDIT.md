# Beyond100 11+/12+ Selective-School Maths Audit

## Purpose

This is separate from the statutory curriculum audit. Selective-school preparation must not distort the canonical curriculum or be presented as statutory content.

The audit asks:
1. What extra content is actually expected?
2. What familiar content is tested at greater depth?
3. What reasoning behaviours are selected for?
4. What exam-format skills matter?
5. Which of these belong in Beyond100 as transferable mathematics rather than school-specific coaching?

## Representative official sources reviewed

- St Paul's Prep School 11+ published mathematics syllabus and current selection procedure.
- St Paul's published mathematics teaching philosophy/problem-solving material.
- Westminster Under School 11+ published mathematics expectations.
- Merchant Taylors' 11+ official specimen mathematics papers and admissions information.
- Habs Boys/Girls current 11+ assessment information.
- 1729 Maths School current admissions material.
- Highgate official 11+ sample mathematics paper.
- Westminster challenge/sample mathematics material as a higher-depth comparator (not treated as an 11+ syllabus).

## Common selective-school pattern

The strongest common finding is **depth, application and unfamiliarity rather than indiscriminate acceleration**.

St Paul's explicitly says its pupils are not accelerated through key stages but are stretched with rich/sophisticated problems and resilience. Its 11+ syllabus requires problem solving, explaining methods/reasoning, pattern recognition, generalisation and investigation while excluding formal algebra from that paper.

Westminster Under School expects completion of KS2 and particularly values applying learnt mathematics in problem-solving contexts.

1729 explicitly states that Stage 2 does not test knowledge beyond the National Curriculum; it focuses on problem solving and mathematical exploration.

Habs currently says its Maths/English content is based on KS2, delivered in a timed multiple-choice format with Maths combined with NVR.

Merchant Taylors combines a conventional Mathematics paper with a General Paper designed to probe creativity/lateral thinking, and its specimen Maths papers include equations/number properties beyond a purely routine KS2 worksheet style.

## Transferable selective-school skills

These should exist as cross-cutting Beyond100 tags rather than isolated "11+ tricks".

### Decode
- identify the action and target;
- distinguish useful from distracting information;
- parse comparative/relational wording;
- notice hidden constraints.

### Represent
- bar model;
- number line;
- table/grid;
- diagram;
- equation/missing-number statement;
- organised list/tree;
- working backwards state.

### Search systematically
- enumerate without omission/duplication;
- split into cases;
- bound the possibilities;
- eliminate impossible cases;
- exploit parity/divisibility/symmetry.

### Reverse reasoning
- reconstruct an original quantity;
- invert a sequence of operations;
- infer missing dimensions/values from output;
- reason from a rounded/averaged/fractional result back to possibilities.

### Structural arithmetic
- factor rather than brute-force;
- use distributivity;
- use complements/differences;
- exploit ratio/scaling;
- recognise invariant totals or differences.

### Pattern/generalisation
- describe what changes and what stays fixed;
- formulate a rule;
- test edge cases;
- distinguish a plausible pattern from a proved rule.

### Explanation
- state why a method works;
- compare two methods;
- justify that all cases have been covered;
- identify a counterexample to a false claim.

### Timed-exam control
- read before calculating;
- move on strategically;
- estimate/check;
- manage multi-choice distractors without guessing from appearance;
- preserve clear working for recovery/checking.

## School-specific observations

### St Paul's 11+
Treat the published syllabus as a particularly useful specification because it states both included and excluded material. Build depth in arithmetic, problem solving, data, measure and geometry, plus explicit explanation/generalisation. Do **not** teach formal algebra merely because it seems "advanced" for this paper.

### Westminster Under 11+
Use secure KS2 as the content floor and emphasise transfer into non-routine problem solving.

### Merchant Taylors'
Maintain strong conventional maths coverage but add creativity/lateral-thinking opportunities separately rather than attempting to "tutor" a fixed General Paper format. Official specimen maths demonstrates that extension algebra/number-structure questions may appear in its published material.

### Habs
Because the current first-round Maths/NVR component is timed and multiple choice, add selective timed sets and distractor analysis **after** underlying concept mastery, never as a substitute for it.

### 1729
Do not create a cram syllabus. Use ordinary mathematics as the substrate for exploration, reasoning, persistence, conjecture, proof and mathematical conversation.

## Coverage verdict

Beyond100 should not have a single bucket called "11+ Quantitative Reasoning" containing disconnected tricks.

Instead:
- content belongs in its canonical maths skill;
- `SEL` marks selective-school application;
- `TIM` marks timed-selection execution;
- `NRT` marks non-routine transfer;
- 1729-style thinking tags record the quality of mathematical thinking.

A question can therefore be tagged:
`fractions.reverse-find-whole + SEL + NRT + STR + PRF`

This lets a weakness discovered in an entrance-paper question improve the underlying curriculum model rather than merely lower an "11+ score".

## Items to add to the catalogue from the selective audit

1. systematic case enumeration;
2. constrained-digit/number construction;
3. invariants and preserved quantities (age-appropriate);
4. reverse chains / working backwards;
5. parity as a reasoning tool;
6. divisibility as a reasoning tool, not only a remembered test;
7. proof of completeness for an organised search;
8. spatial visualisation/nets/rotation as quantitative reasoning;
9. distractor diagnosis and answer-option checking as exam execution metadata;
10. timed fluency under unfamiliar wording as a separate evidence dimension.

## What should NOT become canonical content

- school-specific question templates memorised by appearance;
- arbitrary puzzle "tricks" with no reusable mathematical idea;
- speed techniques that damage conceptual understanding;
- premature algebra/KS3 content solely to appear advanced;
- repeated mock-test drilling before core skill/transfer weaknesses are diagnosed.
